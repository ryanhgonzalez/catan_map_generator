Setup 5

Who goes first

Everyone rolls both dice; the highest roll starts.

Placing your first pieces

In turn order, each player places a settlement and a road touching it. Then the last player goes again and play runs back the other way, so each player places a second settlement and road.

Your second settlement

It can go anywhere the distance rule allows and doesn’t need to connect to your first. Its road must touch it.

Starting resources

Take 1 resource for each land hex touching your second settlement. The last player to place their second settlement takes the first turn.

The robber

It starts the game on the desert.

A turn 4

Roll for resources

Roll both dice. Every settlement next to a hex showing that number collects 1 of its resource, and every city collects 2. The hex with the robber produces nothing.

Trade, then build

After rolling, trade, then build. Once everyone knows the game, the rulebook suggests combining the two: trade and build in any order, as often as you like.

One development card

Play at most 1 per turn, at any point in your turn, even before you roll. It can’t be one you bought this turn.

When the bank runs out

If there aren’t enough of a resource for everyone it’s owed to, no one gets that resource this turn. If only one player is owed it, they take what’s left.

Building 6

Your pieces

Each player has 15 roads, 5 settlements and 4 cities, and can’t build more than that.

Roads

One road per edge. A new road must join one of your roads, settlements or cities.

Settlements

Built on an empty corner touching one of your roads, following the distance rule. Worth 1 point.

Distance rule

None of the 3 corners next to a settlement can have a settlement or city on it, yours included.

Cities

Upgrade one of your settlements; the settlement goes back to your supply. Worth 2 points, and collects 2 resources per roll.

Development cards

Take the top card and keep it hidden. Used cards never go back into the deck.

Development cards 5

Knight

Move the robber and steal, just as on a 7. Played knights stay face up in front of you and count toward Largest Army. There are 14.

Road Building

Place 2 roads for free, following the usual rules.

Year of Plenty

Take any 2 resources from the bank. You can use them this turn.

Monopoly

Name a resource. Every other player gives you all of that resource they hold.

Victory Point

Worth 1 point; keep it hidden. Reveal your Victory Point cards when they take you to 10 on your turn, even the turn you bought one. There are 5.

The robber 3

Rolling a 7

No one collects resources. Anyone holding more than 7 resource cards (8 or more) discards half of them, rounded down.

Moving the robber

Whoever rolled must move it to a different hex (the desert counts), then steals 1 random resource from a player with a settlement or city on that hex. If there are several, they choose who.

A blocked hex

The hex under the robber produces nothing until the robber moves again.

Trading 4

With other players

On your turn, trade with anyone, on any terms you agree. You must be part of every trade; other players can’t trade with each other.

Not allowed

Giving cards away, trading like for like (say, 3 ore for 1 ore), trading development cards, or trading with the bank on someone else’s turn.

With the bank

On your turn, trade 4 of one resource for 1 of any other, harbor or not.

Harbors

With a settlement or city on a harbor: at a 3:1 harbor trade 3 of a kind for 1 of any other; at a 2:1 harbor trade 2 of its pictured resource for 1 of any other.

Longest Road & Largest Army 3

Longest Road

The first player with a continuous road of 5 or more segments takes this card, worth 2 points. Only the single longest branch counts. A player who builds a longer road takes it away.

Breaking a road

A settlement built on an empty corner along someone’s road splits it. If the holder is still tied for longest, they keep the card. If others tie for longest, or no one has 5 or more, the card is set aside until one player has the longest.

Largest Army

The first player with 3 knights face up takes this card, worth 2 points. A player with more knights face up takes it away.

Winning 2

10 points on your turn

Reach 10 points during your own turn and you win at once. If you get there on someone else’s turn, you win once your turn comes.

Points

Settlement 1, city 2, Longest Road 2, Largest Army 2, and 1 for each Victory Point card.

5–6 players 3

Paired turns

The starting player is player 1, and the third player to their left is player 2. Player 1 rolls and takes a full turn. Then player 2 builds, trades with the bank only, and may play a development card. Then both pass their markers to the left.

If both reach 10

Player 1 wins.

Older boxes

Before 2022, 5–6 player games used a special building phase instead: after each turn, everyone else could build in turn order, but not trade.

What’s different 3

The big changes

Cities & Knights plays like CATAN, plus commodities, knights, city improvements, progress cards and the barbarians. There are no development cards and no Largest Army, and you play to 13 points.

Setup

Each player places a settlement and a road, as usual. Then, going back the other way, each places a city (not a settlement) and a road. Your starting resources come from the hexes around your city.

Before you start

The robber starts on the desert, and the barbarian ship starts at the beginning of its track.

A turn 5

Roll 3 dice

Roll the red, yellow and event dice. Resolve the event die first: a ship moves the barbarians 1 space closer; a colored city gate means players may draw progress cards of that color.

Production

Add the red and yellow dice for production as usual. A city on forest, pasture or mountains collects 1 resource plus 1 commodity; on fields it collects 2 grain, and on hills 2 brick.

Then, in any order

Trade, build, hire, activate, promote or use knights, buy city improvements, and play any number of progress cards.

Rolling a 7

No one collects. Anyone holding too many cards discards half, rounded down. The limit is 7 cards, plus 2 for each city wall you have. Then the robber moves and steals, as usual.

Before the first attack

Until the barbarians first reach Catan, a 7 still makes players discard, but the robber stays on the desert and no one steals. Knights and progress cards can’t move it either.

Commodities 2

What they are

Paper from forest, cloth from pasture and coin from mountains. Only cities produce them.

How they work

They count as cards in your hand for a 7, can be stolen or discarded, and trade just like resources: 4:1 with the bank, or 3:1 at a 3:1 harbor. You spend them on city improvements.

Building 3

What things cost

See the costs card above. Roads, settlements and cities cost the same as in CATAN.

Your pieces

Each player has 5 settlements, 4 cities, 15 roads, 6 knights (2 each of basic, strong and mighty) and 3 city walls.

City walls

Built only under a city: one per city, 3 at most. Each one raises your safe hand limit on a 7 by 2. If the barbarians pillage that city, the wall is removed.

Knights 7

Hiring a knight

Pay wool and ore to place a basic knight, inactive, on an empty corner connected to your roads. Knights ignore the distance rule. Basic knights have strength 1, strong 2 and mighty 3.

Activating

Pay 1 grain to turn a knight active. You can activate it the turn you hire it, but a knight can’t act on the turn it was activated. Taking an action turns it inactive again.

Promoting

Pay wool and ore to raise a knight one level, active or not; each knight at most once per turn. Promoting a strong knight to mighty needs the Fortress (politics level 3).

Blocking roads

Other players can’t build roads through a corner with your knight on it, and a knight placed on a road breaks it for Longest Road.

Moving

Move an active knight along your own roads to another empty corner. It becomes inactive. To build a settlement where your knight stands, move the knight first.

Displacing

Move an active knight onto a corner held by a weaker opposing knight. Its owner moves it to an empty corner connected by their roads, or removes it if there’s none. Your knight becomes inactive. You can’t displace your own knights.

Chasing away the robber

An active knight next to the robber’s hex can move the robber and steal 1 card, as with a knight card in CATAN. The knight becomes inactive.

The barbarians 6

The ship

Each time the event die shows a ship, the barbarian ship moves 1 space. When it reaches the last space, the barbarians attack.

The battle

The barbarians’ strength is the number of cities on the island, metropolises included. Catan’s strength is the total of all active knights: basic 1, strong 2, mighty 3.

If the barbarians win

Whoever has the lowest total of active knights loses a city: it’s reduced to a settlement, and its wall is removed. Tied players each lose one. Players with no cities, or only metropolises, can’t lose one and don’t count as lowest.

If Catan wins

The player with the highest total of active knights gets a Defender of Catan card, worth 1 point. If players tie, no one gets it; each tied player draws a progress card from the stack of their choice.

After an attack

The ship goes back to the start, and every knight on the board turns inactive.

Losing your last city

You keep your city improvements and progress cards, but can’t buy improvements until you have a city again.

City improvements 5

Three tracks

Trade (yellow, paid in cloth), politics (blue, coin) and science (green, paper). Each level costs that many of the commodity: 1 for the first, 2 for the second, up to 5. You need at least one city to buy them.

Drawing progress cards

Each level adds red die faces to that track. When the event die shows that color’s city gate and the red die matches one of your faces, you draw a progress card of that color. Players draw in turn order, starting with whoever rolled.

Level 3 abilities

Trading House (yellow): trade commodities 2:1 for anything on your turn. Fortress (blue): promote strong knights to mighty. Aqueduct (green): when a production roll other than a 7 gives you nothing, take any 1 resource.

Metropolis

The first to reach level 4 in a track puts that track’s metropolis on one of their cities. That city is worth 4 points and can’t be pillaged. Reaching level 5 first takes a metropolis from a player at level 4; at level 5 it’s yours for good.

Going past level 3

You can only buy level 4 or 5 in a track if you have a city that could hold a metropolis.

Progress cards 3

Holding them

Keep them hidden; you can hold at most 4, not counting victory point cards. If you draw a 5th on someone else’s turn, put one of your choice under its stack. They can’t be traded, and the robber can’t steal them.

Playing them

Play any number on your turn, after rolling, even ones drawn this turn. Only the Alchemist is played before rolling. Played cards go face down under their stack.

Victory point cards

The Printer and Constitution are played face up as soon as they’re drawn, even on someone else’s turn.

Science cards 10

Alchemist (2)

Play before rolling: choose what the red and yellow dice show (even a 7), then roll the event die and resolve it first.

Crane (2)

One city improvement costs 1 commodity less. You can’t use 2 Cranes on the same improvement.

Engineer (1)

Build a city wall for free.

Inventor (2)

Swap any 2 number tokens on the board, except the 2, 12, 6 and 8.

Irrigation (2)

Take 2 grain for each fields hex next to at least one of your settlements or cities.

Medicine (2)

Upgrade a settlement to a city for 2 ore and 1 grain.

Mining (2)

Take 2 ore for each mountains hex next to at least one of your settlements or cities.

Printer (1)

1 victory point. Play it face up as soon as you draw it.

Road Building (2)

Build 2 roads for free.

Smith (2)

Promote up to 2 knights one level each for free. Mighty knights can’t be promoted, and strong to mighty still needs the Fortress.

Politics cards 9

Bishop (2)

Move the robber, then steal 1 random card from each player with a settlement or city on its new hex.

Constitution (1)

1 victory point. Play it face up as soon as you draw it.

Deserter (2)

An opponent removes one of their knights, their choice. You may then place one of your own of the same strength.

Diplomat (2)

Remove an open road (one with nothing at one end). If it’s your own, you may place it somewhere else for free.

Intrigue (2)

Displace an opponent’s knight on a corner connected to your roads, without using a knight of your own.

Saboteur (2)

Each player with as many points as you or more discards half their cards, rounded down.

Spy (3)

Look at another player’s progress cards and take 1 (not a victory point card).

Warlord (2)

Activate all your knights for free.

Wedding (2)

Each player with more points than you gives you 2 resource or commodity cards of their choice.

Trade cards 6

Commercial Harbor (2)

Offer each opponent 1 resource from your hand; each must give you 1 commodity of their choice for it, if they have one.

Master Merchant (2)

Look at the hand of a player with more points than you and take 2 resource or commodity cards.

Merchant (6)

Put the merchant on a land hex next to your settlement or city. While it stays there, trade that hex’s resource 2:1, and you hold 1 victory point. Another Merchant card takes it over.

Merchant Fleet (2)

This turn, trade one resource or commodity of your choice 2:1 with the bank, as often as you like.

Resource Monopoly (4)

Name a resource. Each other player gives you 2 of it, or 1 if that’s all they have.

Trade Monopoly (2)

Name a commodity. Each other player gives you 1 of it.

Winning 2

13 points on your turn

Reach 13 points during your own turn and you win.

Points

Settlement 1, city 2, metropolis 2 more (4 for that city), Longest Road 2, and 1 each for Defender of Catan cards, the Printer, the Constitution and holding the merchant.

Optional rules 3

Easy start

For each player’s first 2 turns, reroll any 7 and don’t roll the event die.

Knight-errant

An active knight may chase away the robber before you roll, like playing a knight card before rolling in CATAN.

Nasty variant

When the barbarians attack, each player, starting with whoever rolled, decides which of their active knights defend. Only those count, for the battle and for who loses a city.

5–6 players 2

Paired turns

As in CATAN 5–6: player 1 rolls and takes a full turn, then player 2 trades with the bank only, builds, uses knights, improves cities and plays progress cards.

Activating knights

A knight activated during player 2’s part of the turn can act on its owner’s next turn.
